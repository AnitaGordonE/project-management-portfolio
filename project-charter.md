# Project Charter — Digital Product Launch

**Document status:** Draft for portfolio demonstration  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Project duration:** 12 weeks  
**Project sponsor:** Executive Sponsor (role placeholder)  
**Project manager:** Project Manager (case-study role)

> This is a fictional portfolio scenario. Roles and assumptions are illustrative.

## 1. Background and business need
The organisation intends to launch a digital platform to improve how its target users access services and complete key transactions. A coordinated delivery approach is required to align business, product, technology and operational teams.

## 2. Project purpose
Plan and coordinate the design, build, validation and launch of a minimum viable product (MVP) within a 12-week delivery window.

## 3. Objectives and measurable outcomes
- Confirm and baseline the MVP scope by the end of Week 2.
- Obtain business approval of designs before development hand-off.
- Complete development and system testing by the end of Week 10.
- Complete launch readiness checks and obtain go/no-go approval by Week 11.
- Deploy in Week 12, subject to acceptance criteria and release approval.

## 4. High-level scope
**Included:** discovery and requirements, UX/UI design, development coordination, quality assurance, user acceptance testing, launch preparation, deployment coordination and initial hypercare.

**Excluded:** post-launch roadmap beyond the agreed hypercare period, major platform rebuilds, unapproved integrations and expansion into additional markets.

## 5. Key deliverables
1. Approved project charter and scope.
2. Requirements baseline and prioritised backlog.
3. Approved UX/UI designs.
4. Configured and developed MVP.
5. Test plan, test evidence and UAT sign-off.
6. Operational and support readiness pack.
7. Go-live approval and deployment record.
8. Post-launch review.

## 6. High-level milestones
| Milestone | Target |
|---|---|
| Charter and team alignment | Week 1 |
| Scope and requirements baseline | Week 2 |
| Design approval | Week 5 |
| Development complete | Week 8 |
| QA and UAT complete | Week 10 |
| Go/no-go decision | Week 11 |
| Go-live | Week 12 |

## 7. Governance and decision-making
- Sponsor: approves business case, material scope/budget changes and go/no-go decision.
- Project Manager: owns integrated plan, coordination, reporting, RAID log and escalation.
- Product Manager: owns product priorities and acceptance of product requirements.
- Engineering Lead: owns technical estimates, architecture and engineering delivery.
- QA Lead: owns test approach, defect reporting and test exit recommendation.
- Operations Lead: owns support readiness and operational handover.

## 8. Assumptions and constraints
**Assumptions:** named team members are available; stakeholders provide feedback within agreed review windows; required environments and access are provisioned; scope is limited to an MVP.

**Constraints:** fixed 12-week target; cross-functional dependencies; limited capacity; release is dependent on testing, security/privacy review where applicable, and operational readiness.

## 9. Key risks
- Requirements or approvals are delayed.
- Development effort exceeds estimates.
- Integration or environment issues delay testing.
- Critical defects remain unresolved.
- Support and launch communications are not ready.

See `risk-register.md` for scoring and response plans.
